What is a GST & Tax Invoice?

A tax invoice is a specialized billing document issued by a registered commercial enterprise that states the applicable Goods and Services Tax (GST), Value Added Tax (VAT), or Sales Tax collected on behalf of government revenue authorities.

Unlike a standard commercial bill or proforma estimate, a registered tax invoice allows your commercial clients to claim input tax credits (ITC in India, GST credits in Australia, or input VAT in the UK and European Union).

Mandatory Information on a Legal Tax Invoice

Major revenue authorities (ATO, HMRC, GST Council) require seven specific declarations:

  • Document Title: Must prominently display the exact phrase Tax Invoice.
  • Seller Tax ID: Official business tax registration number (ABN in Australia, 9-digit VAT number in the UK, 15-digit GSTIN in India).
  • HSN / SAC Codes: In jurisdictions such as India, professional services and product lines require Harmonized System of Nomenclature (HSN) or Services Accounting Code (SAC) identifiers.
  • Date of Issue: The calendar date establishes the relevant tax reporting quarter.
  • Line Item Details: Description of deliverables, unit prices, and explicit declaration of exempt vs taxable supplies.
  • Explicit Tax Line: Distinct dollar amounts of tax collected, broken down into federal and provincial components where applicable.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does India intra-state vs inter-state tax work?
When supplying within the same state (Intra-State), 18% GST splits into 9% CGST (Central) and 9% SGST (State). For supplies between different states (Inter-State), full 18% IGST is charged.
Where should I display my ABN or VAT registration?
Place your tax ID directly inside your business header block right below your company trading name, so accounts payable clerks can immediately verify your credentials.
Do I charge VAT on cross-border international services?
In most jurisdictions (e.g. UK to US or EU B2B), exported digital services are zero-rated or handled via Reverse Charge. Check with your accountant regarding international supply exemptions.
